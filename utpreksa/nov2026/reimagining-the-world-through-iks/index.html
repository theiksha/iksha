Taking IKS to the World: A Conversation with Dr. Manohar Shinde

Reimagining the World Through IKS: Reflections on institution-building, the global relevance of Indian civilizational wisdom, and creating a sustained intellectual movement.

Interviewee Dr. Manohar Shinde M.D., Ph.D. • Chief Patron, IKSHA
Academic Profile UCLA & ICP Faculty (Retd.) Medical Director & Philanthropist
Focus Verticals 5 IKSHA Initiatives Diiksha, AAC, Sangama, Samiksha, JIKS
Vision Horizon Next 25 Years Civilizational Confidence & De-colonisation

Dr. Manohar Shinde, M.D., Ph.D. Psychiatry, was on the teaching clinical faculty of UCLA & USA schools of Medicine and at the “Institute for contemporary psychoanalysis” (ICP). He has also served as a Medical Director in many institutions in Glendale, Los Angeles. Beyond his professional achievements, Dr. Shinde has dedicated himself to philanthropy and community development, positively impacting thousands of lives.

As Chief Patron of IKSHA (Indian Knowledge System and Heritage Association), Dr. Shinde provides guidance and support to initiatives dedicated to advancing Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS). His engagement with civilizational and academic forums, including initiatives associated with the Dharma Civilization Foundation and Hindu studies programmes, reflects his commitment to bringing India's intellectual and cultural heritage into contemporary academic discourse.

Through his professional experience, philanthropic leadership, and commitment to India's civilisational legacy, Dr. Shinde represents a bridge between modern academic engagement and the enduring wisdom of Indian traditions. In this conversation, Dr. Shinde reflects on the global relevance of Indian Knowledge Systems and shares what inspired him to transform his vision for IKS into a sustained movement after returning to India.

Q

How has your background as a Swayamsevak of Sangh shaped your approach to institution-building?

A

The foundational values instilled by the Sangh taught me that any monumental mission must never be centered on an individual, but rather anchored in a collective vision and a higher civilizational purpose. Selflessness, discipline, consistency, and the spirit of carrying everyone along are the greatest gifts of this training. When we build institutions, they are not merely physical spaces or administrative frameworks; they are living carriers of a civilizational ethos. These core principles have consistently inspired me to build sustainable institutions rooted in indigenous values that foster positive societal transformation.

“When we build institutions, they are not merely physical spaces or administrative frameworks; they are living carriers of a civilizational ethos.”

Q

What did your experience with the Dharma Civilization Foundation (DCF) teach you about the global relevance of IKS?

A

Living and working in the United States for years, and engaging with civilizational discourse through initiatives like the Dharma Civilization Foundation in USA, made me realize that while the modern Western world excels materially, it faces deep psychological, existential, and spiritual vacuums. It is actively searching for holistic frameworks, and it looks toward India’s ancient knowledge traditions with great anticipation. Yoga, Vedanta, and the Dharmic worldview are not merely historical relics; they offer timeless solutions to the complex crises confronting modern humanity. My experiences abroad demonstrated that there is a global hunger for studying India’s civilizational insights.

Q

What motivated you to build IKS as a sustained movement after returning to India?

A

Following my commitment to work in Bharat for 3.5 years, I was suggested by Shri Dattatreya Hosabale, the Sarkaryavah of RSS, to focus on strengthening the IKS movement. It was very evident that our own indigenous knowledge systems had suffered decades of systematic neglect, and reviving them as an alternative to dominant paradigms across multiple domains was an important imperative of our times. Drawing from my experience overseas, I recognized that we cannot merely take pride in our ancient past on an emotional level; that heritage must be formally mainstreamed into global knowledge production and modern academia. Transforming the pursuit of our traditional knowledge from fragmented study into a vibrant, robust, and structured intellectual movement through platforms like IKSHA became my core mission.

Q

How did you address the initial challenges when NEP 2020 opened up opportunities for IKS?

A

While NEP 2020 opened up an unprecedented window of opportunity, the initial challenges were twofold: first, the lingering risk of IKS being viewed narrowly as ritualistic or archaic, and second, the technical challenge of institutionalizing it academically through collaboration with individuals and institutions in India and abroad. Our strategy has been clear: to establish an ecosystem for rigorous capacity building, supporting future-generation scholarship. Even though there has been a large number of conferences and teacher training efforts in the IKS space, there was not always adequate attention to quality, rigor, and pursuit of excellence.

Q

What was the objective of your extensive visits to universities over the past three years?

A

The primary objective of this extensive travel was to foster a nationwide intellectual networking regarding IKS by engaging stakeholders across academia, business, and government. These interactions revealed that young researchers and professors across the country are remarkably eager to re-examine their civilizational heritage. What they needed was structural institutional support, rigorous research frameworks, and collaborative networks—a reality strongly validated by these nationwide engagements. Hence we decided to focus on in-depth scholarship and credible new knowledge production. In December 2023, an IKS Sangama was held in Pune with over 80 serious IKS stakeholders, helping foster a broader IKS ecosystem, community fraternity, and collaborative spirit.

Q

How did you bring together diverse scholars to build a meaningful IKS ecosystem?

A

No large-scale movement is ever built by a single individual; it requires weaving a dedicated Vidya Parivara (family of scholars) connecting experts across diverse domains. I was aware of the value of collaborative networking. Through domain-specific working groups, we identified, engaged, and united brilliant minds from science, technology, history, literature, and philosophy around a shared civilizational vision. When personal egos are set aside in service of a collective intellectual mission, a resilient ecosystem naturally takes shape.

Q

What are the flagship programmes and initiatives of IKSHA?

A

Indian Knowledge Systems and Heritage Association (IKSHA) is a Section 8 company. It operates through five major verticals that form a comprehensive, self-sustaining ecosystem:

01

Diiksha

108 PhD & Postdoctoral Initiative

A flagship capacity-building initiative aiming to support 108 doctoral and postdoctoral researchers across IKS dimensions over the next 10 years to build future thought leaders.

02

Annual Academic Conference

Intergenerational Academic Dialogue

Fosters intergenerational dialogues among academic scholars and initiates younger scholars into research. Two conferences hosted (JNU & GBU Greater Noida); the 3rd at IIT Bombay in July 2027.

03

IKSHA Sangama

Stakeholder Conclave

Brings together practitioners, scholars, spiritual leaders, industry, and government. Following editions at DES Pune, Gujarat Univ & SSSIHL Bengaluru, the next will be at Amrita University, Kerala.

04

Samiksha

Multidisciplinary Research Consortia

Interdisciplinary collaboration meeting series connecting scientists, technologists, artists, and traditional scholars. Hosted 50 scholars at JNU in May 2026; 6 joint projects funded with ₹90 lakh.

05

Journal of IKS (JIKS)

Peer-Reviewed Brill Publication

High-quality international peer-reviewed journal published by Brill, providing a credible platform for global scholarship, led by Chief Editor Prof. Jyotirmaya Tripathy (former HOD, IIT Madras).

Q

What transformation do you hope to see in Indian education and society over the next 25 years?

A

Over the next 25 years, I envision an Indian higher education system and society that is completely liberated from mental colonisation. I want to see a future where our students and researchers do not simply imitate Western paradigms, but draw inspiration from our indigenous texts to position IKS as a viable alternative across multiple domains—solving contemporary global challenges like environmental sustainability, mental health, and ethical leadership through intellectual self-respect and civilizational confidence.

Q

What is your larger vision for IKS globally, and your message for the next generation?

A

My vision is crystal clear: to establish a platform of excellence that positions IKS as an alternative to the dominant global paradigm. Whether through mainstreaming IKS to produce knowledge globally, training the public through immersive experiences, or building rigorous PhD and postdoctoral scholarship, the goal remains the universal welfare inherent in Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam. The upcoming generation of scholars and institution-builders must treat this knowledge not merely as a museum of history, but as a guiding compass for the future, carrying this civilisational beacon forward with absolute integrity and academic excellence.
